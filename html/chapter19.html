Getting Your Site Out There

You've spent the last 19 chapters learning how to build a website. This final chapter is about what happens next — getting your site onto the internet, making sure people can find it, and understanding the basics of how the web works from a practical standpoint.

Search Engine Optimisation — SEO

SEO stands for Search Engine Optimisation. It's the practice of helping search engines like Google understand what your site is about so it appears in relevant search results.

There are two types of SEO work — on-page and off-page.

On-page SEO is about the content and structure of your actual pages. The key areas to focus on as a beginner are:

  • Page title — the text in your title tag. This appears in search results and browser tabs. It's one of the most important on-page signals.
  • URL — short, descriptive, and readable URLs help both users and search engines understand what a page is about.
  • Headings — search engines give extra weight to text in heading tags. Use them to clearly describe what each section contains.
  • Text — use words people would actually type into a search engine when describing your content.
  • Link text — the words inside your a tags. "Click here" tells search engines nothing. "HTML beginner tutorial" tells them exactly what the linked page is about.
  • Image alt text — search engines can't see images but they can read alt text. Use it to describe images clearly and naturally.
  • Meta description — appears under your page title in search results. Write one that makes people actually want to click.

Off-page SEO is mostly about links from other websites pointing to yours. Focus on on-page SEO first.

Analytics — Understanding Your Visitors

Once your site is live, you'll want to understand who's visiting it and how they're using it. Google Analytics is the most widely used free tool for this.

Once installed it gives you data on:

  • How many people visit your site and when
  • Where they come from — search, social media, direct, or other sites
  • Which pages they visit and in what order
  • How long they spend on each page
  • What device and browser they're using
  • Where they drop off and leave

Domain Names

A domain name is your web address — the thing people type in to find your site. Domain names are registered through a domain registrar — popular ones include GoDaddy, Namecheap, and Google Domains. You pay an annual fee to keep your domain, typically between £10 and £30 per year.

Tips for choosing a domain name:

  • Keep it short and easy to spell
  • Avoid hyphens and numbers if possible
  • .com is still the most trusted extension globally
  • Get your name as a domain as early as you can — good names go quickly

Web Hosting

To put your site on the internet, your files need to live on a web server. You rent space on that server from a web hosting company.

  • Shared hosting — your files share a server with many other websites. Cheapest option, fine for small sites.
  • Virtual Private Server (VPS) — you get a dedicated portion of a server. More control and better performance.
  • Dedicated server — you rent an entire physical server. Expensive, but maximum control.
  • Cloud hosting — your site runs across multiple servers. Flexible and scalable.

For a beginner personal site, shared hosting or a platform like Netlify is completely sufficient and often free.

FTP and Third-Party Tools

FTP stands for File Transfer Protocol. It's a way of transferring files from your computer to a web server. To use FTP you need an FTP client — FileZilla is the most popular free one.

In the paid section of this guide we'll use Netlify instead of FTP — which is even simpler. You drag your project folder into the browser and it deploys instantly. But it's worth understanding FTP because you'll come across it in real web development work.

🔨 Now Let's Build!

Like Chapter 18, this chapter doesn't have a CSS build section. Instead, there are two practical things worth doing right now.

⚡ Task 1 — Add a Meta Description to Your Page

Open your index.html in Notepad and add this inside your head tags:

<meta name="description" content="I am learning HTML and CSS and this is my first webpage." />

Save and refresh. You won't see it on the page — it lives in the head. But search engines will read it.

⚡ Task 2 — Think About Your Domain Name

You don't need to buy one right now but start thinking about it. Go to namecheap.com or godaddy.com and search a few options — it's free to search, you only pay if you buy.

For a personal portfolio, yourfirstname-lastname.com or yourfirstnamelastname.com is always a solid starting point.

✅ Your index.html head section should now look like this:

<head>
  <title>My First Webpage</title>
  <meta name="description" content="I am learning HTML and CSS and this is my first webpage." />
  <link href="css/styles.css" type="text/css" rel="stylesheet" />
</head>
