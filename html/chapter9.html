A Quick Note on Flash

If you've ever come across old websites with animations, video players or interactive games, there's a good chance they were built using Flash. For many years Flash was the main way to add video and audio to websites. However Flash is now completely defunct — all major browsers dropped support for it in 2020 and it no longer works anywhere. The good news is that HTML5 gives us much better built-in tools for video and audio anyway.

Adding Video to Your Page

When it comes to adding video to your site you have two main options — use a hosted service like YouTube or Vimeo, or host the video yourself using the HTML5 video element.

The easiest way is to upload your video to YouTube or Vimeo and use the embed code they provide. You simply paste it into your HTML and the video player appears on your page. No need to worry about file formats or encoding — they handle all of that for you.

HTML5 Video

HTML5 introduced the video element which lets you host video directly on your own site. The browser provides its own built-in player.

  • src — the path to your video file
  • poster — an image to show while the video loads or before the user hits play
  • width and height — the size of the player in pixels
  • controls — tells the browser to show its built-in play/pause controls
  • autoplay — starts playing automatically. Use sparingly!
  • loop — starts again from the beginning once it finishes
  • preload — none, auto, or metadata
<video src="video/puppy.mp4"
       poster="images/puppy.jpg"
       width="400"
       height="300"
       controls>
  <p>Your browser does not support this video.</p>
</video>

Video Formats

Not all browsers support the same video formats, so it's best practice to provide your video in more than one format. The two main formats to cover are MP4 and WebM. Always put MP4 first.

<video poster="images/puppy.jpg" width="400" height="320" controls>
  <source src="video/puppy.mp4" type="video/mp4" />
  <source src="video/puppy.webm" type="video/webm" />
  <p>Your browser does not support this video.</p>
</video>

HTML5 Audio

The audio element works in the same way as video but for sound files. The most widely supported format is MP3.

<audio controls>
  <source src="audio/test-audio.mp3" />
  <source src="audio/test-audio.ogg" />
  <p>Your browser does not support this audio.</p>
</audio>

As with video you can use multiple source elements to provide the audio in different formats. MP3 has the widest support so always include that one.

🔨 Now Let's Build!

Open your index.html file in Notepad. For this chapter we're going to add a video section. If you don't have a video file handy, use a YouTube embed instead — go to any YouTube video, click Share, then Embed, and copy the iframe code they give you.

Add this inside your content div:

<h2>A Video I Like</h2>
<!-- paste your YouTube embed code here -->
<!-- OR if you have a video file: -->
<video width="400" height="300" controls>
  <source src="video/myvideo.mp4" type="video/mp4" />
  <p>Your browser does not support this video.</p>
</video>

Save and refresh your browser. 🚀
